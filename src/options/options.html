Tab Out 2 settings

Choose what sits at the top of your dashboard and how tabs get grouped.

Pinned sites

Shown inside each site's own domain card — a pinned site with an open tab looks like any other tab there. One with no open tab still shows up in that same card as a grayed-out, click-to-open placeholder, instead of getting a card of its own. Turning this off stops applying the list without deleting it.

Label applies to the pinned page and anything under its path. Pin a site root (https://example.com/) to label every page on it — handy when a site redirects you somewhere deeper after loading. Pin a specific page (https://example.com/team/board) to label only that page, leaving other tabs on the same site with their own titles.

Disposable tabs

Tabs matching these rules are safe to close, because reopening them costs nothing — either a site's own homepage (X feed, GitHub front page), or a spent one-off page like the screen a Zoom meeting leaves behind once the call has already opened in the app. They're collected into one Disposable card, and count toward the dashboard's "Tidy up" button. Turning this off stops applying the rules without deleting them.

Pattern: matched against the tab's whole URL, scheme included. * matches any characters, including / · https://github.com/* matches every page on GitHub · https://github.com/*/issues/* matches any issue page on any repo · *.google.com/* matches any page on any Google subdomain · file:///Users/me/notes/* matches every local file under that folder · a bare * matches every URL.

Tab sorting

Whether your tabs should be reordered to match the dashboard automatically, or left alone until you click "Sort tabs" on the banner yourself. Either way, sorting only ever tidies plain tabs: your Chrome tab groups and pinned tabs stay exactly where you put them, and a plain tab is never moved across a group.

Auto-grouping

When on, opening 2 or more tabs that would share one domain card automatically collects them into a real Chrome tab group, named after that domain. Any tab you open later on that same site joins that group instead of starting a second one. A tab you've already put in a Chrome tab group yourself is never re-grouped or moved.

Keyboard shortcut

Opens and closes the Search overlay on the Tab Out 2 page — search across your open tabs and recently closed history, move the selection with the arrow keys, and press Enter to jump to a tab or reopen a closed one. Only active on the Tab Out 2 page itself.

Global shortcuts

Work anywhere in Chrome, not just on the Tab Out 2 page. The key combination itself is owned by Chrome, so it's changed in Chrome's own shortcut settings, not here — both are already on by default with the recommended keys below.

Cmd+Shift+O — opens the Tab Out search box over the page you're on, and closes it again on a second press — identical to clicking the toolbar icon. This is Chrome's own "activate the extension" shortcut.

Cmd+Shift+T — opens the Tab Out dashboard, even when Tab Out isn't your new tab page.

Chrome keeps some combinations for itself — Ctrl+Shift+T (Windows/Linux) and Cmd+Shift+T (Mac) both normally reopen your last closed tab, so either one may already be taken and Tab Out's binding shown as blank until you reassign it below. A combination shown as blank in Chrome may also mean another extension has claimed it.

History

How many recently closed tabs to remember. The oldest entries drop off automatically once you go over this limit. Reopening a tab, or a page you already have open, is never counted as history.
